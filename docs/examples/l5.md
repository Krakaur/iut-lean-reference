# Worked model: `l = 5`

## Coordinate layer

`Fl 5` has five values. Negation pairs `1` with `4` and `2` with `3`, while
zero is fixed.

## Multiplicative quotient

Only the nonzero coordinates are used:

```text
{1,4}  {2,3}
```

T04 therefore returns two classes.

## Additive sign quotient

Including zero gives:

```text
{0}  {1,4}  {2,3}
```

T05 returns three classes. Its countermodel additionally shows that the chart
`x ↦ 2*x` respects negation but is not one of the two allowed representatives
generated by post-negation.

## Signed-affine multiplication

Let `g=(1,true)` and `h=(2,false)`, where `true` means negative. Then

```text
g*h = (1-2, negative) = (4,true).
```

The direct-product mutation would give translation three.

## Action orientation

At `z=3`, first applying `h` gives `3+2=0`; applying `g` next gives one. The
product `(4,true)` also sends three to `-3+4=1`. This is T07's right-to-left
composition check.

## Cardinal comparison

The signed-affine carrier has `5*2=10` elements. Thus the same model displays
three different sizes: multiplicative quotient `2`, additive quotient `3`, and
affine group `10`.

## Translation subgroup and dihedral convention

The positive-sign pairs form the translation subgroup. The reflection
`q=(0,true)` lies outside it and right multiplication by `q` toggles membership,
which witnesses the index-two decomposition used in T09.

For T10, the negative pair `(1,true)` must map to the dihedral constructor
`sr(-1)`. Acting at `z=2` then gives four, in agreement with the signed-affine
action. The superficially plausible map to `sr(1)` gives two and is rejected.
