## Plain mathematical translation

The signed-affine group on `l` coordinates is the dihedral group of a regular
`l`-gon. The comparison is an isomorphism, not a definition: both groups are
constructed independently and then related.

With Mathlib's constructor convention, the map is

```text
(a,+) ↦ r a
(a,-) ↦ sr (-a).
```

The minus sign in the reflection coordinate is forced by multiplication.

## Lean architecture

```lean
def signedAffineDihedralEquiv (l : ℕ) :
    SignedAffine l ≃* DihedralGroup l

def dihedralAct : DihedralGroup l → Fl l → Fl l
  | r a,  z => z + a
  | sr a, z => -z - a

theorem T10_C06_dihedral_action_compatible (g) (z) :
    dihedralAct (signedAffineDihedralEquiv l g) z = g.act z
```

Multiplicativity is checked in all four sign cases. The dihedral action is
defined directly from `r` and `sr`; it is not transported from the
signed-affine action. Compatibility is therefore a genuine comparison theorem,
not a definitional tautology.

## Closed example and discriminator

At `l=5`, let `g=(1,-)` and `z=2`. The correct image `sr (-1)` sends `z` to
`4`, agreeing with the signed-affine action. The tempting map to `sr 1` sends
the same coordinate to `2`. `T10_example_l5_action_discriminant` proves all
three values.

## Priority and limits

The abstract dihedral comparison is standard mathematics, and Takkun already
uses `DihedralGroup l` as an IUT-oriented model. The contribution here is the
visible convention audit and executable rival test. T10 does not identify the
dihedral group with a geometric automorphism or Galois group.
